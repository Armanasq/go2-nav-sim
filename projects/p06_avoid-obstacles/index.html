Sense and avoid · Project 6

Reactive obstacle avoidance without a map

Goal

Walk the Go2 from marker A to marker B around the arena obstacles with no map and no Nav2. The node reads only the 2D scan (/scan) and its own pose estimate (/odom), and on every scan picks a direction to walk in. You write two ways of picking it: a potential field, which adds a pull towards the goal to a push away from every nearby return, and a simplified vector field histogram, which takes the free corridor nearest to the goal. You tune both in a 2D model of the arena first, then run at least five trials each way in Gazebo, score them on ground truth (success, time, path length, minimum clearance), compare them with the Nav2 runs of chapter 11, and make each method fail in a way you can explain.

What you will learn

  • Turning a LaserScan into points the controller can reason about, and dropping the returns that mean "nothing seen".
  • A potential field: the pull, Khatib's push, and why their sum can be zero in front of an obstacle (a local minimum).
  • A free-corridor test: which directions are clear for a robot of a given width, and how to pick one of them.
  • Why a reactive choice needs memory (hysteresis), and the traps that it still walks into.
  • Placing a goal given in the world frame into the odometry frame once, at the start, and living with odometry drift after that.
  • Tuning against a cheap 2D model before spending simulator time, and checking where the model and Gazebo disagree.

Before you start

You need project 1 (starting the stack, /odom_gt, why only a zero twist stops CHAMP), project 3 (/odom drifts while the robot stands), project 4 (steering on /odom, wrap, reading a pose from nav_msgs/Odometry) and project 5 (bearings of a LaserScan, inf returns, range_min = 0.4 m).

Every terminal starts with:

cd go2-nav-sim
source env.sh

This project needs the simulator only. Nav2 must not run (it would also publish on /cmd_vel), and the estimation stack must not run either, because the project's launch file starts its own copy for every trial (step 4 says why). If scripts/up.sh is running, stop it with scripts/stop.sh first.

The arena is defined in src/go2_nav_sim/scripts/make_arena.py: walls at x = ±6 and y = ±5 (inner faces at ±5.9 and ±4.9), four boxes, two cylinders, A = (-4.5, -3.5) and B = (4.0, 3.0). The two boxes that matter most here are the long brick box centred at (1.0, 0.0), 0.8 × 2.4 m, and the pallet at (2.5, -2.5), 1.2 m square. The gap between their nearest corners is 0.86 m. /odom_gt is relative to A, so world = /odom_gt + A.

File What it is

arena2d.py the arena in 2D: a ray-cast /scan, distance to the nearest obstacle, a kinematic robot that runs reactive.py

How the code works

From a scan to points

def valid_returns(ranges, angle_min, angle_inc, range_min, range_max):
    r = np.asarray(ranges, dtype=float)
    a = angle_min + angle_inc * np.arange(len(r))
    ok = np.isfinite(r) & (r >= range_min) & (r <= range_max)
    return r[ok], a[ok]

As in project 5: beam i points at angle_min + i * angle_increment in base_footprint (x forward, y left), inf means no return, and values outside [range_min, range_max] are unusable. Here the whole 360 degrees is used: 901 beams, 0.4 degrees apart. range_min is 0.4 m, so anything closer than 0.4 m to the scan origin is invisible. Both methods below keep the robot centre at least 0.5 m from everything for that reason.

The potential field and its local minimum

def apf_direction(r, a, g, angle_inc, p=P):
    att = p['k_att'] * np.asarray(g) / max(math.hypot(*g), 1e-9)
    near = r < p['d0']
    d, b = r[near], a[near]
    mag = p['k_rep'] * (1 / d - 1 / p['d0']) / d ** 2 * angle_inc
    f = att - np.array([np.sum(mag * np.cos(b)), np.sum(mag * np.sin(b))])
    return math.atan2(f[1], f[0])

The pull has constant size k_att and points at the goal g (in the robot frame). Each return at distance d < d0 pushes straight away from itself with

|push| = k_rep (1/d - 1/d0) / d²

which is minus the gradient of Khatib's potential ½ k_rep (1/d - 1/d0)². Each beam's push is multiplied by its angular width, so a wall pushes as hard at 0.4 degree spacing as at 1 degree. With d0 = 1.5 m and k_rep = 1.0 a wall starts to matter 1.5 m away and the push grows like 1/d³ close to it.

The robot walks along the sum. A local minimum is a place where the sum is zero. With the goal straight behind the brick box, the pull is +x and, by symmetry, so is the whole push, reversed: the y parts of the push from the upper and lower halves of the face cancel. At some distance the two are equal. In my Gazebo run apf_wall the robot reached that point 0.76 m from the face and stopped making progress. The direction of a near-zero sum is set by noise, so the log shows it flipping from side to side:

t       x      y      v     w      th
20.06  -0.18   0.07   0.00  -0.50  -2.65
27.64  -0.21  -0.10   0.00   0.50   2.60

(columns t, gt_x, gt_y, v, w, th of results/apf_wall.csv). The second failure in step 2, between the brick box and the pallet, is the same balance with two obstacles: their pushes cancel the pull in a gap of 0.86 m between corners. Nothing in the field says which way round is shorter, so the method has no way out by itself. The unit test test_apf_symmetric_wall_points_straight_at_it checks the symmetric case: the direction comes out as exactly 0.

Free corridors (the histogram)

def free_directions(r, a, look, r_safe, n_dir):
    t = np.linspace(-math.pi, math.pi, n_dir, endpoint=False)
    rel = a[None, :] - t[:, None]
    along, side = r * np.cos(rel), np.abs(r * np.sin(rel))
    blocked = ((along > 0) & (along < look) & (side < r_safe)).any(axis=1)
    return t, ~blocked

For each of 180 candidate bearings t (2 degrees apart), each return is split into a distance along t and a distance across it. The bearing is blocked if any return lies ahead within look = 2.0 m and closer than r_safe = 0.55 m to the centre line: the robot, walking that way, would pass within 0.55 m of it. That is the polar histogram of VFH reduced to a yes or no per bearing, with the robot's width built in (Nav2's footprint radius for this robot is 0.33 m, config/nav2_params.yaml, which leaves 0.22 m of margin). It is a matrix of 180 × up to 901 numbers per scan: 7.6 ms per call on my loaded machine, with a scan every 100 ms.

def vfh_direction(r, a, g, prev=None, p=P):
    dist = math.hypot(*g)
    t, free = free_directions(r, a, min(p['look'], dist), p['r_safe'], int(p['n_dir']))
    if not free.any():
        return None
    ang = lambda x: np.abs(np.arctan2(np.sin(t - x), np.cos(t - x)))
    cost = ang(math.atan2(g[1], g[0])) + p['k_turn'] * np.abs(t) + (0 if prev is None else p['k_prev'] * ang(prev))
    cost[~free] = np.inf
    return float(t[np.argmin(cost)])

Of the free bearings it takes the cheapest:

cost(t) = |t - goal bearing| + k_turn |t| + k_prev |t - last choice|

The second term prefers what is already ahead (k_turn = 0.3), the third keeps the last choice (k_prev = 0.5). The look-ahead is cut to the distance to the goal, so a wall behind the goal does not block it. Without the third term my first Gazebo version flipped between two gaps of nearly the same cost (chosen bearing +1.75 rad, then -2.83 rad, then back) and turned on the spot until it was declared stuck after 25 s. step() takes the last choice in world coordinates and turns it into the robot frame, so the choice survives the robot turning. Choosing a corridor 0.55 m wide also closes the 0.86 m gap between the brick box and the pallet, which is where the potential field got caught.

The look-ahead mattered most in tuning. On six starts around A (±0.3 m, ±20 degrees) the 2D model reached B six times with look 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 m, but with 1.5 m it went south round the pallet on three of them (15.5 to 15.8 m), and with 2.0 m it took the 11 m route on five.

From a direction to a command

def command(th, front, p=P):
    if th is None:
        return 0.0, p['w_max']
    w = max(-p['w_max'], min(p['w_max'], p['k_w'] * th))
    brake = min(1.0, max(0.0, (front - p['stop']) / (p['slow'] - p['stop'])))
    return p['v_max'] * max(0.0, math.cos(th)) * brake, w

Both methods give a bearing th in the robot frame. The robot turns towards it at w = 1.2 th, limited to CHAMP's 0.5 rad/s, and walks at 0.3 m/s × cos th: full speed when th = 0, nothing when the direction is 90 degrees or more to the side. front is the nearest return within ±35 degrees of straight ahead; the speed falls linearly from full at 1.2 m to zero at 0.5 m. CHAMP keeps walking on the last command, so the node sends a command on every scan and five zeros at the end.

Placing the goal, and the stuck rule

if self.anchor is None:                                  # once: where the odom frame sits in the world
    self.anchor, self.t0 = reactive.compose(self.gt, reactive.inverse(self.odom)), t
pose = reactive.compose(self.anchor, self.odom)

The goal is given in the world frame, /odom starts wherever the estimator was started. On the first scan, the node takes the true pose from /odom_gt once and computes the transform T = T_world_base · T_odom_base⁻¹ that places the odom frame in the world. From then on the pose is T · /odom. This is the same shortcut go_to_goal.py takes to start AMCL (top-level README). After that /odom_gt is only logged. Without a map nothing corrects the odometry, so its error goes straight into where the robot stops (odom_err_end in the RESULT line).

def stuck(self, t, dist):
    if self.t_best is None or dist < self.best - self.eps:
        self.best, self.t_best = dist, t
    return t - self.t_best > self.window

The run is declared stuck when the distance to the goal has not dropped 0.25 m below its best value for 30 s. This cannot tell a local minimum from a long detour that first leads away from the goal. With a 20 s window the 2D model declared a run stuck that would have arrived (from (-1, 0) to (3, 0) with look = 1.5 m it gave up after 26.6 s; with 30 s it arrives after 35.7 s, --start -1 0 0 --goal 3 0 --set look=1.5); 30 s was enough for the detours in this arena.

What the numbers say:

  • From A to B the reactive navigator arrived in all six runs, and five of them took almost the route Nav2 plans on the map (10.83 to 10.97 m against Nav2's 11.31 and 11.37 m in w1 and w2). It was faster: 38.6 to 40.2 s against 50.9 and 51.5 s. Nav2's controller is set to desired_linear_vel: 0.25 m/s (config/nav2_params.yaml) and slows down in turns tighter than 0.9 m radius (use_regulated_linear_velocity_scaling); this node walks at 0.3 m/s whenever the way ahead is clear.
  • The minimum clearance was 0.56 to 0.57 m in every reactive run: the robot always passed one obstacle as close as the corridor half-width (0.55 m) allows. Nav2 kept 0.62 m in w1 and w2 and 0.51 m in w3. Measured from the robot centre; with the 0.33 m footprint radius the closest the body came was about 0.24 m.
  • B to A is 5 m longer than A to B. The robot arrives on B facing about +25 degrees, and with that start heading the histogram goes south down the east side of the arena and west along the south wall. In the 2D model the same happens: arena2d.py --start 3.75 2.9 25 --goal -4.5 -3.5 gives 15.63 m, while the same start facing west (--start 4 3 180) gives 10.89 m. Without a plan the route is decided by where the robot happens to be facing.
  • The robot stopped 0.23 to 0.36 m from the goal: the 0.3 m tolerance on its own estimate plus an odometry error of 0.02 to 0.18 m. Nav2 has the same kind of tolerance (0.25 m) and corrects its odometry with AMCL; this node cannot, and over longer routes the error would grow (project 3 measures the drift).
  • The one potential-field run from A to B (apf_ab1) arrived, although the 2D model of step 2 predicted a stall between the brick box and the pallet. The Gazebo run started facing 180 degrees (it had just walked back to A); the model from that pose (--method apf --start -4.26 -3.62 180) also arrives, after 11.49 m. The local minimum is there, but the robot only falls into it from some starting headings.
  • Six and three runs are small samples. One histogram failure in twelve legs is not a failure rate.

Where the histogram gets trapped

All three failures in the right panel of the figure are traps: places where every free bearing points away from the goal and the cheapest one changes as the robot turns.

  • ba4, 29 s into the run, beside the pallet at (3.7, -3.1). In ba3 the robot stood in the same place (3.71, -3.04) and chose a bearing of -40 degrees, round the pallet's south-east corner; 4.5 s later it was walking west below the pallet. In ba4 it chose -49 degrees, then, half a second later, +91 degrees: the corridor round the corner and the corridor back north cost almost the same, a few degrees of heading tipped it, and k_prev then held it on the way back. It walked north into the space between the brick box's east face and the pallet, where the brick box blocks the way to A, and turned there until the stuck rule ended the run at (2.52, -0.12).
  • vfh_wall started where apf_wall gave up, facing south (-76 degrees). It walked south past the end of the brick box into the corner between the box and the pallet (closed to it, since 0.86 m < 2 × 0.55 m) and turned there; the chosen bearing went -2.83, -1.57, -0.49, -2.79 rad over 7.5 s. The 2D model from (-1, 0) facing +x went north round the box instead and arrived (Try this, exercise 2).
  • apf_wall is the potential-field minimum, explained above.

A reactive method sees only the current scan. It cannot know that a corner is closed until it is in it, and it has no memory of where it has been, so it can walk back into the same place. Nav2 plans on the whole map and does not have this problem in this arena; that is the price of having no map.

Try this

  1. Halve the push and predict where the potential field stalls in front of the brick box.

  1. Move the goal 1 m to the side and show that the minimum needs symmetry.

bash python3 projects/p06_avoid-obstacles/arena2d.py --method apf --start -1 0 0 --goal 3 1
