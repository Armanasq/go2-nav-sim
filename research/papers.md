# Key papers for a practical legged-robot course (Go2, ROS 2)

Scope: one to three papers per topic that a practitioner needs, with the code that goes with them. Checked on 2026-11-28.

How each entry was checked:
- **V**: the abstract page (arXiv, publisher, or repository record) or the README was opened in this session, and title, authors and year were matched against Crossref or arXiv.
- **S**: metadata confirmed on Crossref, but the content summary comes from a search snippet or from the authors' code rather than from the paper.
- Repository numbers (stars, last push) come from the GitHub API on 2026-11-28.

Where this course stands now (from `docs/`): CHAMP gait controller, a lidar-inertial odometry front end (CHAMP leg odometry is disabled because Gazebo publishes no foot contacts, ch. 9), `robot_localization`, `slam_toolbox`, AMCL and Nav2. The papers below are grouped by the layer they explain or by the gap they fill.

---

## 1. Legged state estimation

**Bloesch, Hutter, Hoepflinger, Leutenegger, Gehring, Remy, Siegwart. "State Estimation for Legged Robots - Consistent Fusion of Leg Kinematics and IMU." RSS VIII, 2012.** https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss08/p03.html (V)
This is the founding kinematic-inertial filter. It is an observability-constrained EKF that propagates with the IMU and adds the stance foothold positions to the state, so leg kinematics become measurements of the base relative to a foot assumed fixed. It assumes no terrain geometry. *Takeaway:* leg odometry is "the stance foot does not move", and every failure (slip, wrong contact flag) breaks that assumption. *Course slot:* the theory box in ch. 9, explaining why CHAMP's leg odometry fails when `/foot_contacts` is missing.

**Hartley, Ghaffari, Eustice, Grizzle. "Contact-aided invariant extended Kalman filtering for robot state estimation." IJRR 39(4), 2020.** https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364919894385, arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09251 (V). The RSS 2018 conference version is arXiv 1805.10410 (cited in the repo README; abstract page not opened).
This filter keeps the same contact-inertial model as Bloesch but writes the state on a matrix Lie group, so the error dynamics do not depend on the current estimate. The paper reports better convergence and a more consistent observability model than a quaternion EKF, tested on Cassie. Code: `RossHartley/invariant-ekf` (C++, Eigen only, 572 stars, last push 2019-08). It ships `src/examples/kinematics.cpp` for the contact-aided case; the ROS 1 wrapper `invariant-ekf-ros` has 62 stars and is also from 2019. *Takeaway:* the InEKF is the filter to use when you write your own leg odometry; it removes most of the EKF tuning around large initial heading error. *Course slot:* an optional advanced exercise in ch. 9: fuse Go2 joint encoders, IMU and foot contact into a base-velocity estimate.

**Camurri, Ramezani, Nobili, Fallon. "Pronto: A Multi-Sensor State Estimator for Legged Robots in Real-World Scenarios." Frontiers in Robotics and AI 7:68, 2020.** https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2020.00068 (V, metadata and README)
Pronto is a modular EKF that combines IMU and leg odometry with optional lidar and camera pose corrections. It was used on Atlas, HyQ and ANYmal. Repo: `ori-drs/pronto` (329 stars, last push 2026-06). The README says the `main` branch was ported to ROS 2 Humble in November 2025 (University of Pisa). It has a `pronto_quadruped` leg-odometry module and depends on Pinocchio. There is also a `pronto_anymal_example` to copy from. A companion paper explains how contacts are obtained without foot sensors: Camurri et al., "Probabilistic Contact Estimation and Impact Detection for State Estimation of Quadruped Robots," RA-L 2(2), 2017, https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2017.2652491 (metadata V, content not read). *Takeaway:* Pronto is the only maintained, legged-specific open estimator found that runs on ROS 2 Humble, which is the course's distribution. *Course slot:* ch. 9, as the "fix leg odometry properly" path in place of CHAMP's estimator.

**Wisth, Camurri, Fallon. "VILENS: Visual, Inertial, Lidar, and Leg Odometry for All-Terrain Legged Robots." IEEE T-RO 39(1):309-326, 2023.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07243 (V)
VILENS is a fixed-lag factor graph that tightly fuses all four sensor types. It estimates a linear-velocity bias on the leg-odometry factor to absorb slip and leg flexibility. On ANYmal, the paper reports about 62% lower translational and 51% lower rotational error than a loosely coupled baseline. No public code was found. *Takeaway:* leg odometry drifts in a structured way (a velocity bias), and that bias can be estimated online. Tight coupling matters most when one sensor degrades. *Course slot:* a reading in ch. 9, explaining why this course's lidar-only odometry is a deliberate simplification.

**Gap filler: Yang, Zhang, Fu, Manchester. "Cerberus: Low-Drift Visual-Inertial-Leg Odometry For Agile Locomotion." arXiv 2209.07654, 2022 (submitted to ICRA 2023).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07654 (V)
Cerberus is open visual-inertial-leg odometry built from one stereo camera, an IMU, joint encoders and contact sensors. It adds online kinematic calibration and contact outlier rejection, and reports drift below 1%. Repo: `ShuoYangRobotics/Cerberus` (384 stars, last push 2023-07, ROS 1, Docker). It ships bags from a Unitree A1 (260 m) and a Unitree Go1 (350 m). *Takeaway:* this is the closest open legged estimator on a Unitree platform; leg length calibration and contact rejection are what make it work. *Course slot:* ch. 9 reading and a dataset for a Go1/Go2-style leg-odometry exercise.

**Gap filler: Xu, Cai, He, Lin, Zhang. "FAST-LIO2: Fast Direct LiDAR-Inertial Odometry." IEEE T-RO, 2022.** https://doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2022.3141876 (S: metadata only. The course already cites arXiv 2107.06829; this session did not reopen that abstract.) Repo `hku-mars/FAST_LIO`: 5,427 stars, last push 2026-10. *Takeaway:* this is the reference lidar-inertial odometry to compare the course's own lidar odometry against. *Course slot:* ch. 9 "Where this sits".

## 2. Locomotion control

**Di Carlo, Wensing, Katz, Bledt, Kim. "Dynamic Locomotion in the MIT Cheetah 3 Through Convex Model-Predictive Control." IROS 2018.** https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2018.8594448, author PDF at https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/138000.2 (V)
The paper models the trunk as a single rigid body, linearises it around the commanded yaw, and plans ground reaction forces as a convex QP. It uses horizons up to 0.5 s, solved in under 1 ms at 20-30 Hz. It covers stand, trot, flying trot, pronk, bound, pace, a 3-legged gait and gallop, with one set of gains, reaching up to 3 m/s forward. Code: `mit-biomimetics/Cheetah-Software` (3,342 stars, last push 2023-10), in `user/MIT_Controller/Controllers/convexMPC`. *Takeaway:* the MPC plans forces, not joint angles, and the single-rigid-body model is enough for walking and trotting. *Course slot:* ch. 1 "The layers" and ch. 7. Contrast it with CHAMP, which has no force planning.

**Kim, Di Carlo, Katz, Bledt, Kim. "Highly Dynamic Quadruped Locomotion via Whole-Body Impulse Control and Model Predictive Control." arXiv 1909.06586, 2019.** https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06586 (V)
Convex MPC plans reaction forces over a long horizon. A whole-body impulse controller (WBIC) then turns them into joint torques at a higher rate, using the full model. On Mini Cheetah it reaches 3.7 m/s across six gaits. Code: the same repo, `Controllers/WBC/WBIC` and `WBC_Ctrl`. *Takeaway:* this is the standard two-rate split: a slow MPC on a simple model, then a fast whole-body QP on the full model. *Course slot:* ch. 1 layer diagram, as background.

**Wensing, Posa, Hu, Escande, Mansard, Del Prete. "Optimization-Based Control for Dynamic Legged Robots." IEEE T-RO, 2024.** https://doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2023.3324580, arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11644 (V)
This survey covers how optimal-control problems for legged robots are made fast enough for real time: how contact is modelled, which reduced models are used, and how those choices shape the solver. It also discusses combining these methods with learning. *Takeaway:* use it as the map of the model-based control field, to read when a learner asks "what comes after CHAMP?". *Course slot:* further reading in ch. 1 and ch. 7.

**What the course's controller is actually based on.** The CHAMP README says its controller follows Jongwoo Lee, "Hierarchical controller for highly dynamic locomotion utilizing pattern modulation and impedance control: implementation on the MIT Cheetah robot," MIT S.M. thesis, 2013, https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/85490 (V). That design uses virtual leg compliance, force distribution and a gait-pattern modulator, tested at about 6 m/s trotting on a treadmill. Repo `chvmp/champ`: 2,343 stars, last push 2024-07. The README lists ROS Kinetic and Melodic only. *Course slot:* ch. 7. Learners should know that CHAMP is an open-loop gait pattern with impedance and has no contact-force planning.

## 3. Perception for legged robots

**Fankhauser, Bloesch, Hutter. "Probabilistic Terrain Mapping for Mobile Robots With Uncertain Localization." RA-L 3(4):3019-3026, 2018.** https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2018.2849506 (S: metadata from Crossref, abstract from search snippets; full text not opened)
This paper builds a robot-centric 2.5D elevation map from range data and a drifting proprioceptive pose. Each cell carries upper and lower confidence bounds that grow with pose uncertainty. Repo `ANYbotics/elevation_mapping`: 1,911 stars. The README says it is no longer actively maintained, the repo is archived, and it is ROS 1 only. Its map type, `ANYbotics/grid_map` (3,278 stars, pushed 2026-11), is still maintained. *Takeaway:* a legged robot needs height, not a 2D occupancy grid. Map in the robot frame so that odometry drift blurs old cells instead of corrupting the whole map. *Course slot:* a new section after ch. 10, "From a 2D costmap to an elevation map".

**Miki, Wellhausen, Grandia, Jenelten, Homberger, Hutter. "Elevation Mapping for Locomotion and Navigation using GPU." IROS 2022, pp. 2273-2280.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12876 (V. The venue comes from the repo's BibTeX, because the arXiv page lists none.)
This is the GPU successor to the 2018 package. It adds height-drift compensation, ray-casting visibility cleanup, a learned traversability filter and plane segmentation, and it was used in the DARPA SubT Challenge. Repo `leggedrobotics/elevation_mapping_cupy`: 1,182 stars, last push 2026-11-27. The README still documents a catkin and ROS 1 install. Several ROS 2 branches exist (`dev/ros2/*`, `dev/jj/g1/ros2_humble`, `codex/elevation-despike-jazzy`), but none were opened, so ROS 2 status is unverified. *Takeaway:* this is the elevation mapper actively used on legged robots today; plan around a CUDA GPU. *Course slot:* the same new section, as the tool to run.

**Miki, Lee, Hwangbo, Wellhausen, Koltun, Hutter. "Learning robust perceptive locomotion for quadrupedal robots in the wild." Science Robotics 7(62), 2022.** https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.abk2822, arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08117 (V)
An attention-based recurrent encoder learns when to trust the elevation map and when to fall back on proprioception (snow, vegetation, glare). With it, ANYmal finished an hour-long alpine hike in the time suggested for human hikers. *Takeaway:* exteroception for footholds is unreliable. Controllers that work outdoors treat the map as a noisy hint, not as ground truth. *Course slot:* reading in the elevation-map section and the learning chapter.

## 4. Navigation

**Macenski, Martin, White, Gines Clavero. "The Marathon 2: A Navigation System." IROS 2020.** https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS45743.2020.9341207, arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00368 (V)
This is the Nav2 design paper: behaviour-tree orchestration, lifecycle nodes, and planner and controller servers on ROS 2, tested on a campus among students. Repo `ros-navigation/navigation2`: 5,120 stars, pushed 2026-11-27. A companion paper covers what each Nav2 plugin does: Macenski, Moore, Lu, Merzlyakov, Ferguson, "From the Desks of ROS Maintainers: A Survey of Modern & Capable Mobile Robotics Algorithms in ROS 2," RAS 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15236 (V). *Takeaway:* Nav2 treats the Go2 as a holonomic or differential base on a 2D costmap, and the rest of this list is what that hides. *Course slot:* ch. 10, required reading. The survey goes in the Nav2 parameter section, to explain the choice of planner and controller.

**Wermelinger, Fankhauser, Diethelm, Krusi, Siegwart, Hutter. "Navigation planning for legged robots in challenging terrain." IROS 2016.** https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2016.7759199 (S: metadata V via Crossref. The ETH PDF did not load because the server returns a JS app, so the method is taken from the authors' code.)
This paper computes traversability from an elevation map and plans over it, tested on StarlETH. The authors' repo `leggedrobotics/traversability_estimation` (522 stars, last push 2024-06, ROS Noetic) shows the actual model in `robot_filter_parameter.yaml`. It combines a slope filter (critical value 1.0), a step filter (critical 0.12 m) and a roughness filter (critical 0.05 m). Traversability is their mean, `(1/3)(slope + step + roughness)`, followed by a footprint check. *Takeaway:* legged traversability is three interpretable terrain features compared against the robot's step height. This can be built as a Nav2 costmap layer without any learning. *Course slot:* a new exercise after ch. 10: turn an elevation map into a Nav2 costmap layer using slope, step and roughness thresholds.

**Gap filler: Wellhausen, Hutter. "ArtPlanner: Robust Legged Robot Navigation in the Field." Field Robotics, 2023.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01420 (V. The DOI 10.55417/fr.2023013 is listed on arXiv; the journal page was not opened.)
This is the planner used by team CERBERUS at the SubT finals. It combines sampling-based pose selection, learned foothold scores and a learned motion cost. *Takeaway:* it shows the current field standard beyond Wermelinger, and that a legged robot can step over things a 2D costmap marks as lethal. *Course slot:* further reading in ch. 10.

**Gap fillers for what the course already runs (V, metadata from Crossref):**
- Moore, Stouch, "A Generalized Extended Kalman Filter Implementation for the Robot Operating System," IAS-13, 2014/2015, https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-08338-4_25. This is the `robot_localization` paper (repo 1,992 stars, pushed 2026-11).
- Macenski, Jambrecic, "SLAM Toolbox: SLAM for the dynamic world," JOSS 2021, https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.02783 (repo 2,736 stars, pushed 2026-11).
- Fox, "Adapting the Sample Size in Particle Filters Through KLD-Sampling," IJRR 22(12), 2003, https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364903022012001. This is the adaptive particle count behind AMCL.

Course slot for all three: one citation each in ch. 9 and ch. 10. AMCL is the weakest part of the current stack (README: 8-19 cm RMSE), so the KLD-sampling paper explains the `min_particles` and `max_particles` and `pf_err` parameters a learner tunes.

## 5. Learning-based locomotion

**Rudin, Hoeller, Reist, Hutter. "Learning to Walk in Minutes Using Massively Parallel Deep Reinforcement Learning." CoRL 2021.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11978 (V)
Thousands of parallel environments on one GPU train ANYmal flat-ground policies in under 4 minutes and rough-terrain policies in about 20 minutes, using a terrain curriculum and PPO. Repo `leggedrobotics/legged_gym`: 3,202 stars, last push 2025-05. Its README says the environments moved to Isaac Lab and the repo gets only limited updates. `isaac-sim/IsaacLab` (8,739 stars, pushed 2026-11-27) has a Go2 velocity task at `source/isaaclab_tasks/.../velocity/config/go2`. `unitreerobotics/unitree_rl_gym` (3,692 stars, pushed 2025-07) is built on legged_gym and rsl_rl. It supports `--task=go2` and documents a Train, Play, Sim2Sim (MuJoCo), Sim2Real flow; its README shows Mujoco and real deployment only for G1/H1. *Takeaway:* an RL walking policy for the Go2 is a GPU-hours exercise, not a research project. The hard part is sim-to-sim and sim-to-real checking, not training. *Course slot:* an optional chapter "Replace CHAMP with a learned policy", using IsaacLab or unitree_rl_gym, with the MuJoCo sim-to-sim step required before Gazebo.

**Ha, Lee, van de Panne, Xie, Yu, Khadiv. "Learning-based legged locomotion: State of the art and future perspectives." IJRR, 2025.** https://doi.org/10.1177/02783649241312698, arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01152 (V)
This survey covers why learning overtook model-based control for quadrupeds (simulation, compute, cheap hardware), the standard recipe (PPO, domain randomisation, teacher-student training, terrain curricula), the move to humanoids, and open problems. *Takeaway:* it is the single reading that explains the vocabulary of every Go2 RL repo. *Course slot:* the opening reading of the learning chapter.

**Margolis, Agrawal. "Walk These Ways: Tuning Robot Control for Generalization with Multiplicity of Behavior." CoRL 2022.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03238 (V)
One policy is conditioned on gait parameters (frequency, phase, body height, foot swing height, and so on), so behaviour can be retuned at run time without retraining. Repo `Improbable-AI/walk-these-ways`: 1,475 stars, last push 2024-06. It targets the Unitree Go1 with Isaac Gym Preview 4 and needs a GPU with at least 10 GB of VRAM by default. Deployment uses `unitree_legged_sdk`, and the README warns to hang the robot up for low-level mode. *Takeaway:* expose gait parameters as commands. This maps directly onto the "Gait parameters" section of ch. 7, where CHAMP's parameters are fixed in YAML. *Course slot:* ch. 7 sidebar and the learning chapter. Porting it to the Go2 is a known but non-trivial step (Go1 SDK vs Go2 `unitree_sdk2`).

## 6. Benchmarks and datasets for legged robots

**Frey, Tuna, Fu, Patterson, Xu, Fallon, Cadena, Hutter. "GrandTour: A Legged Robotics Dataset in the Wild for Multi-Modal Perception and State Estimation." arXiv 2602.18164, 2026 (submitted to IJRR).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18164 (V. Also a vault note, `grandtour.md`.)
The dataset has 49 ANYmal-D missions (over 10 km and over 5 h per search snippet S). It provides synchronised lidar, cameras, proprioception and stereo depth, with RTK-GNSS and total-station reference trajectories. The vault note records a benchmark of 63 open-source estimators under one protocol. Data are on Hugging Face (`leggedrobotics/grand_tour_dataset`) in ROS and ROS-free formats. *Takeaway:* this is the reference legged dataset to score a state estimator against survey-grade ground truth. *Course slot:* an optional "test your estimator on real data" exercise in ch. 9 or ch. 11.

**Ou, Li, Li. "Leg-KILO: Robust Kinematic-Inertial-Lidar Odometry for Dynamic Legged Robots." RA-L, 2024.** https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2024.3440730 (metadata V; the paper was not opened.)
Dataset repo `ouguangjun/legkilo-dataset`: 82 stars, last push 2026-10. It is a Unitree Go1 dataset with joint encoders, contact sensors, IMU and a Velodyne VLP-16 at about 10 Hz. Ground truth comes from a prior map or offline optimisation, and the corridor and slope sequences are closed loops for end-to-end error. Per the README, kinematics are published at 500 Hz but only update at 50 Hz because of an SDK limit, and the repo ships a Go1 leg Kalman filter derived from Cheetah-Software. *Takeaway:* this is the closest public data to the course robot (a Unitree quadruped with lidar). The 50 Hz SDK limit is a real-world caveat worth teaching. *Course slot:* ch. 9 exercise data. Cerberus bags (section 1) are a second Unitree source.

## What a course like ours should take from this

1. **Teach leg odometry as a model with an explicit failure mode.** Add a half-page to ch. 9 deriving base velocity from one stance foot (Bloesch 2012), then show what the current stack shows: no contact flags means no leg odometry. Give one diagnostic plot: leg-odometry velocity vs ground-truth velocity during a trot.
2. **Give learners a way to get contacts without a contact sensor.** Gazebo's contact node was too expensive (ch. 3 patch). A cheap substitute is to threshold the estimated foot force from joint torques, or to use gait phase from CHAMP. Cite Camurri 2017 as the probabilistic version. This unblocks CHAMP's estimator and the item below.
3. **Offer Pronto (ROS 2 Humble since Nov 2025) as the "do it properly" estimator.** It is the one open legged estimator on the course's exact distribution. Do not write a new filter; write a Go2 config modelled on `pronto_anymal_example`. Mark the result as experimental until a run is scored against `/odom_gt` with the existing `bag_metrics.py`.
4. **Keep lidar-inertial odometry as the main path, and say why.** VILENS shows that tight multi-sensor fusion beats any single source, but it has no public code. FAST-LIO2 is the comparator. One table in ch. 9 comparing the course odometry, FAST-LIO2 and (optionally) Pronto on the same bag is worth more than more theory.
5. **Explain the control layer stack honestly.** CHAMP is a gait pattern plus impedance (Lee 2013 thesis). Convex MPC plans ground reaction forces with a single rigid body (Di Carlo 2018, under 1 ms per solve). WBIC turns those forces into torques (Kim 2019). A single diagram in ch. 1 with these three plus "RL policy" covers it; the Wensing survey is the pointer for anyone who wants more.
6. **Add one elevation-map step between the 2D scan and Nav2.** The current height-band scan (ch. 10) hides steps and slopes. Minimum version: run `grid_map` plus an elevation mapper on the RGB-D camera. Then compute slope, step and roughness and their mean exactly as in Wermelinger's `traversability_estimation` config (critical values 1.0, 0.12 m, 0.05 m), scaled to the Go2's smaller step height, and publish it as a costmap layer. Check ROS 2 support of `elevation_mapping_cupy` before committing (only branches exist, unverified).
7. **State what Nav2 assumes about the robot.** Use Marathon 2 and the ROS 2 navigation survey to explain why a quadruped is configured as a planar base, what that loses (stepping over obstacles, ArtPlanner), and which Nav2 parameters stand in for gait limits: velocity limits, footprint and inflation.
8. **Tie AMCL tuning to its paper.** AMCL is the weakest part of the stack (8-19 cm RMSE). Citing Fox 2003 (KLD-sampling) and Moore 2014 (`robot_localization`) gives learners the meaning of the parameters they are changing, rather than trial and error.
9. **Make learned locomotion an optional, bounded chapter.** Use IsaacLab's Go2 velocity task or `unitree_rl_gym --task=go2` (Rudin 2021 recipe, Ha 2025 survey for vocabulary). Make MuJoCo sim-to-sim a required check before Gazebo or hardware. Borrow one idea from Walk These Ways: expose gait parameters as runtime commands, as ch. 7 already does with CHAMP's YAML.
10. **Score on real legged data once.** Add one exercise that runs the course's estimator on a public legged dataset: Leg-KILO (Go1 with lidar, closest to Go2) or GrandTour (survey-grade ground truth). Report ATE and RPE with the existing scripts. This is the only way the course's numbers mean anything beyond Gazebo.

Not included, deliberately: contact-implicit trajectory optimisation, humanoid locomotion, and parkour-level RL. They are outside "a beginner to autonomous navigation".
